**What was the breakthrough that moved the work forward?**

Restarting. ComplyLogic had been growing for weeks around a compliance
procedure nobody could feel, and no amount of polish fixed that it wasn't
satisfying to use. The breakthrough was deciding the claim had to be
something a stranger could feel working within thirty seconds — a table
voting itself into a Presenter and a law — and then writing that claim down
in README.md *before* touching code, so every later decision (one guarded
write per round, triggers that make the record permanent) was a direct
consequence of the claim instead of a judgement call made in isolation.

**What did this work change about who I want to be as a software developer?**

It sharpened the difference between a feature list and a claim. ComplyLogic
had plenty of features and no single sentence anyone could check against the
running app. Writing "what good means here" first, then deriving every
non-negotiable rule from one clause of it, is a discipline I want to keep:
ask what the app is claiming before asking what it does.
